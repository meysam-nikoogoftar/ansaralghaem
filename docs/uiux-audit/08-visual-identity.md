# Visual identity and template-pattern review

2026-11-18 (Asia/Tehran). Source-only findings; no rendered visual assessment or application modification.

## Identity evidence

The existing product supplies concrete identity material: organization name/logo, Persian/RTL language, student-community history, pilgrimage/shrine photography, culturally relevant quotations, Vazirmatn/Aref Ruqaa type roles and recurring deep-green/teal/gold color. Light mode additionally uses geometric/arabesque patterns and varied pale section surfaces. These are meaningful subject cues in source; their final balance cannot be judged without rendering.

No approved brand guide, existing DESIGN.md, audience study or visual regression reference was available. This audit cannot determine fidelity to an undocumented identity standard, and makes no claim about who or what created the website.

## Recommendations tied to evidence

First make the current identity trustworthy: verified registration availability, accurate financial explanation, real channel destinations and operator-facing recovery copy ([F13](10-master-findings.md#f13), [F16](10-master-findings.md#f16), [F17](10-master-findings.md#f17), [F26](10-master-findings.md#f26), [F28](10-master-findings.md#f28)). These strengthen brand credibility without changing its visual world.

Then review hierarchy at actual mobile and desktop sizes. Preserve the local logo, meaningful photography and typographic language. If effects compete with task comprehension, reduce or reassign them selectively based on observed attention/readability. Do not assume gradients, cards, ornament, minimalism or rounded shapes are inherently inappropriate.

Keep public storytelling and operational precision connected through type/color/content voice, while allowing member/admin screens to prioritize scanability, values and state feedback. Choose no new visual template on source evidence alone.

## Validation needed

- First-impression comprehension: can visitors identify the organization, current registration status and next action?
- Mobile readability and which elements receive attention before task-critical information.
- Recognizability across home/auth/member/admin and both themes.
- Whether users understand the boundary between wallet savings, trip payment and charitable participation.
- Whether decorative motion feels useful and remains comfortable with reduced motion enabled.

These are research questions, not findings of actual user confusion. Only F39-F41 are research hypotheses in the consolidated register; this report introduces no separately counted aesthetic defects.
